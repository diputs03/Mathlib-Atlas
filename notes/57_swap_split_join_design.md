## 1. Purpose

Script 56 proved, for every combinatorial map (rotation system), that the Euler
characteristic `χ = V − E + F` is even and that an integer genus `g` exists with
`χ = 2 − 2g`. It explicitly left the inequality `χ ≤ 2` (`g ≥ 0`) for connected
maps unproved, noting that the missing combinatorial primitive is the classical
fact that **multiplying a permutation by a transposition changes its number of
cycles (orbits) by exactly one**. Script 57 proves that primitive in the two
adjacent/fixed-point forms needed by an edge-insertion induction on a map.

Mathlib has the local ingredients (`IsCycle.swap_mul`, `support_swap_mul_eq`,
`IsCycle.cycleType`, `SameCycle.exists_pow_eq'`) but neither a `numCycles`
function nor a split/join orbit theorem, so both are constructed here.

## 2. What is proved

With `orbits α p = Multiset.card p.cycleType + (Fintype.card α − p.cycleType.sum)`
(nontrivial cycles plus fixed points; identical in meaning to the helper of 56,
redefined because the numbered scripts are standalone and cannot import one
another):

- `orbits_one`, `orbits_cycle (hf : IsCycle f) : orbits α f = card α − #support f + 1`,
  `orbits_swap : orbits (swap a b) = card α − 1`.
- **`orbits_adjacent_split`** — if `f` is one cycle and `f x ≠ x`, then
  `g = swap x (f x) * f` satisfies `orbits α g = orbits α f + 1`.
  - length-2 cycle: `f = swap x (f x)` (`eq_swap_of_apply_apply_eq_self`), so `g = 1`;
  - length ≥ 3: `IsCycle.swap_mul` gives `g.IsCycle` and
    `support_swap_mul_eq` gives `g.support = f.support \\ {x}`, hence one more orbit.
- **`orbits_fixed_join`** — if a cycle `f` fixes `z` and `x` is on the cycle
  (`f x ≠ x`), then `h = swap z x * f` is one cycle through `z` and the whole
  support of `f`, with `h.support = insert z f.support`, and
  `orbits α h = orbits α f − 1`.

The join is the exact algebraic inverse of the split: cutting the joined cycle
just after `z` recovers `f` and the fixed point `z`.

## 3. How the join is proved

There is no Mathlib lemma producing a cycle by *absorbing* a fixed point, so the
`IsCycle` for `h` is constructed directly with the `⟨basepoint, moved, ∀ moved y
∃ i : ℤ, h^i basepoint = y⟩` constructor, basepoint `z`.

- `h z = x`; the support is shown pointwise to be exactly `insert z f.support`
  (a point fixed by `f` other than `z,x` stays fixed; points on the cycle move;
  the closing predecessor of `x` maps to `z`).
- Writing `n = orderOf f = #f.support`, minimality of the period at the moved
  point `x` follows from `IsCycle.pow_eq_one_iff'` and
  `orderOf_dvd_of_pow_eq_one`: `f^j x = x` with `0 < j < n` would force `n ∣ j`.
- Induction gives `(h^(j+1)) z = (f^j) x` for `j < n`; every point of
  `f.support` is `f^j x` for some such `j` (`SameCycle.exists_pow_eq'`), so every
  moved point of `h` lies on the single `h`-orbit of `z`.

## 4. Honest scope

Proved here:

1. adjacent-cut **split** of one cycle (cycle length 2 and ≥ 3 separately), with
   the exact `+1` orbit count;
2. fixed-point **join** of one cycle with one fixed point, with the exact `−1`
   orbit count.

Not proved (not needed for the immediate map argument; noted in the file header):

- the cut between two arbitrary points of the same cycle;
- the join of two arbitrary nontrivial cycles.

Both follow by the same `f^i x` orbit enumeration used in `orbits_fixed_join`
(conjugate the cut/join points onto the adjacent/fixed positions); they are left
for the edge-insertion proof that consumes them.

## 5. What this unlocks

Together with 56, the remaining combinatorial topology is now:

1. **connectedness** of a map — transitivity of the subgroup generated by the
   edge involution `τ` and the vertex rotation `σ`;
2. an **edge-insertion induction** in which each insertion is one split or join
   of the vertex/face permutations; the `±1` lemma of 57 then bounds the change
   in `χ`, yielding `χ ≤ 2` / `g ≥ 0` for connected maps;
3. genus-zero **realization** by a plane/sphere drawing — the remaining
   topological layer that would make the `PlaneData` interfaces of 51/53/54
   concrete.

Items 1–2 are pure combinatorics over the now-proved primitive; item 3 is the
genuine (topological) layer gap, still cited rather than proved.
